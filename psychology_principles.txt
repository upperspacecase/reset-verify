PRINCIPLE 1: Feedforward
  Why it fits: A sysadmin improvising verification per call needs to see exactly what happens on both ends of the next MFA-reset call before they will say "I'd run it," which is the DM reply we are testing for.
  How to apply: Make the hero a three-frame storyboard of one call, agent side and caller side, with a timer running 0:00 to 0:58: frame 1 "You paste one link into the ticket or chat," frame 2 "Caller scans ID + live selfie on their phone," frame 3 "You get a green check and an audit-log row with timestamp and ticket ID." No marketing headline above it, the storyboard is the headline.

PRINCIPLE 2: Anchoring Bias
  Why it fits: The 1-3 person IT team and the MSP service-desk lead already carry the anchor "identity verification means an enterprise contract and a sales call," so the per-check price is the thing that makes them believe this is for them rather than for the 5,000-seat company.
  How to apply: Place the per-check price directly under the storyboard in one line, "$X per check. No contract, no minimum, no sales call," and put the three negations in the same visual weight as the price; do not show a tier table or any monthly plan, and leave the per-check figure as the only number on the page.

PRINCIPLE 3: Spark Effect
  Why it fits: Reddit traffic is skeptical and skims, so hitting 10 attributable sign-ups depends on the commit step costing almost nothing at the moment they are convinced by the storyboard.
  How to apply: Use a single email field with a button reading "Send me the link when I can run a check," no name, company, or seat-count fields, placed immediately under the price line and repeated once at the page bottom; add a ?ref=sysadmin and ?ref=msp parameter on the two Reddit comment links and store it with the signup so each of the 10 can be attributed.
